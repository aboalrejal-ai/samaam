## 2. Contrast-Induced Nephropathy & Renal Safety Thresholds

### 2.1 International Clinical Guideline (ESUR/ACR thresholds referenced)

- **document_title**: Guideline on the use of iodinated contrast media in patients with kidney disease (consensus text referencing ESUR/ACR thresholds)  
- **issuing_authority**: Japanese Society of Nephrology et al. (international clinical guideline)  
- **section_reference**: CQ3‑1 (Renal function; CIN)  
- **content**: “There is very little evidence that IV iodinated contrast material is an independent risk factor for AKI in patients with an eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m2. If any threshold for CIN risk is to be used, 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 seems to be the one with the greatest level of evidence.”  
- **source_url**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6949208/  
- **publication_year**: 2019  
- **language**: English  

### 2.2 RANZCR / eviQ CI‑AKI risk by eGFR

- **document_title**: eviQ / RANZCR‑linked guideline – CI‑AKI risk by eGFR  
- **issuing_authority**: Cancer Institute NSW (citing RANZCR)  
- **section_reference**: Table 1.0 “Risk of CI‑AKI based on eGFR”  
- **content**: “>45 mL/min/1.73 m2: Likely non‑existent … 30–45 mL/min/1.73 m2: Very likely to be low or non‑existent … <30 mL/min/1.73 m2 (severe renal function impairment)… Highest risk of CI‑AKI.”  
- **source_url**: https://www.eviq.org.au/clinical-resources/radiation-oncology/contrast-administration/4299-contrast-media-renal-adverse-events  
- **publication_year**: 2023  
- **language**: English  

### 2.3 UCSF / ACR‑aligned CT contrast guideline

- **document_title**: UCSF / ACR‑aligned CT contrast guideline  
- **issuing_authority**: UCSF Radiology (summarizing ACR Manual)  
- **section_reference**: eGFR‑based table  
- **content**: “At the current time, there is very little evidence that intravenous iodinated contrast material is an independent risk factor for AKI in patients with eGFR ≥ 30 mL/min/1.73 m2… Patients with chronic kidney disease whose eGFRs are <30 mL/min/1.73 m2 may be at risk for developing post‑contrast acute kidney injury… Contrast should not be administered unless … benefits outweigh the risk.”  
- **source_url**: https://radiology.ucsf.edu/patient-care/health-professionals/patient-safety-healthcare-professionals/ct-and-x-ray-contrast-guide  
- **publication_year**: 2025

### 2.4 Multi‑society summary including ESUR and ACR (metformin)

- **document_title**: Metformin and contrast – multi‑society summary including ESUR and ACR  
- **issuing_authority**: UIC Drug Information Group (table of guidelines)  
- **section_reference**: ACR 2023 and ESUR 2018 rows  
- **content**: “ACR (2023): Patients with eGFR ≥30 and no evidence of AKI: no need to discontinue metformin either prior to or following IV iodinated CM… Patients with severe CKD (stage IV or V [eGFR <30]) or AKI… temporarily discontinue metformin… ESUR (2018): Patients with eGFR >30… continue metformin; Patients with eGFR <30… stop taking metformin… measure eGFR within 48 hours, and restart metformin if renal function has not changed significantly.”

## REGULATORY GAPS

### 1. eGFR Thresholds and Contrast Dose Limits in KSA

- No publicly available Saudi primary text (SFDA or NRRC) sets explicit numeric eGFR thresholds (e.g., 30 and 45 mL/min/1.73 m2) for iodinated contrast administration in CT; widely cited thresholds derive from international guidelines (ESUR, ACR, RANZCR) and are therefore UNVERIFIED as Saudi statutory text.  
- The Saudi Ministry of Health document “Protocols on the Safe Use of Contrast Media in Radiology Departments” is confirmed by title and citation, but the full PDF is not publicly accessible; consequently, numeric eGFR thresholds, hydration protocols, and metformin handling rules cannot be verified from the primary Saudi source.  

### 2. CT Radiation Dose Limits and DRLs in KSA

- SFDA has announced “National Diagnostic Reference Levels” and references MDS‑G008 as the national DRL instrument, but detailed CT dose tables (CTDIvol, DLP per examination) are not available in the publicly accessible text; thus, no numeric Saudi DRLs for CT examinations can be confirmed from primary sources.  
- NRRC‑R‑01 establishes justification and optimisation principles for medical exposure but does not, in the available text, provide numeric dose limits specific to oncology patients undergoing repeated CT follow‑up; emphasis is on occupational and public exposure limits.  

### 3. Regulation of Clinical Decision Support Software and Liability

- SFDA regulates SaMD and AI/ML software as “medical devices” based on intended use, focusing on manufacturer requirements for MDMA (technical file, verification/validation, risk management, QMS), but the available text does not contain explicit provisions on physician or technologist liability when relying on algorithmic recommendations to modify CT protocol parameters or contrast dosing.  
- PDPL regulates health data as sensitive data and mandates processing impact assessments (Article 22), but the available text does not explicitly address “solely automated decision‑making” or a data subject’s right to object to algorithmic clinical recommendations, nor does it allocate liability among developer, healthcare facility, and clinician in such scenarios. Secondary reports mention a right to object, but this is not verifiable from the primary PDPL text accessed.  

### 4. Integration of Health Information Exchange (HIE) Policies with AI Systems

- No primary text from the Ministry of Health, Saudi Health Council, or NHIC was found that establishes a detailed framework for exchanging medical imaging and contrast data across HIE networks with AI‑specific requirements (e.g., data provenance, annotation quality, secondary use restrictions). Available materials focus on general PDPL compliance without a specialized health AI layer.  

### 5. Gaps in AI‑CT Governance for Routine Practice

- No Saudi text explicitly addresses scenarios such as a fatigued CT technologist accepting an AI recommendation to adjust mAs/kVp or contrast dose, followed by patient harm (e.g., contrast‑induced AKI, excessive radiation dose). Allocation of responsibility among technologist, supervising physician, healthcare facility, and device manufacturer is not specified in published law or regulation.  
- WHO ethics guidance emphasizes autonomy, accountability, and transparency, but these principles are not yet incorporated into binding Saudi regulatory frameworks; MDS‑G010 and PDPL do not explicitly reference WHO principles as mandatory requirements, leaving a gap between international best practices and local statutory obligations.
